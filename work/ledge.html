01Why I built it

Screenshots were piling up faster than I could use them.

Designing all day means capturing all day: a reference from a website, a bug in a build, a state I want to compare later. On a Mac each one lands on the Desktop with a timestamp for a name, and copied images vanish the moment I copy something else.

I kept stopping mid-flow to dig through the Desktop, rename files and clean up afterwards. I wanted every capture to wait in one place, one drag away from whatever I was working in. Nothing on the Mac did that the way I wanted, so I built it.

02What it does

A shelf under the menu bar for every capture.

1Catches captures on its own

New screenshots and copied images land on the shelf as they happen, flying up into the menu bar icon.

2Opens from anywhere

Click the Ledge icon in the menu bar or press ⌥ Space. Escape or a click outside puts it away.

3Drags into any app

Pull a thumbnail into a design file, a document or a message. Select several to drag them in together.

4Previews and zooms

Double-click a thumbnail to preview it. Scroll to zoom, drag to look around, arrow keys to step through.

5Keeps what matters

Pin the keepers. Clear removes everything else, and can move the original screenshot files to the Trash.

03What it does for me

Fewer interruptions, one drag at a time.

  • No more Desktop pileCaptures stop cluttering the Desktop. They wait on the shelf until I need them, and go when I clear them.
  • References stay in reachWhile I design, the screens I am comparing sit one keystroke away instead of in a Finder window.
  • Faster write-ups and hand-offsDropping screens into docs, tickets and messages is one drag, several at a time.
  • Nothing to save firstCopied images are kept too, so a quick copy is never lost to the next one.
04Who uses it

It started as mine. Now my colleagues use it too.

After I built Ledge, I showed it to a few colleagues. Some of them now use it every day, for office work like design reviews, bug reports and hand-offs, and for their own personal tasks as well.

Seeing it stick with people beyond me is what pushed me to tidy it up for a public release.

05How I built it

Designed in Figma, built with Claude and Xcode.

I designed the shelf, the thumbnails and the preview in Figma first, then built the native macOS menu bar app in Xcode with Claude as my pair programmer. The demo above recreates the app in the browser so anyone can try it without installing anything.
